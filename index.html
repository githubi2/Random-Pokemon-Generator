      How it works

      A Random Pokémon Generator Built for Real Runs

      Whether you are preparing a Nuzlocke run, hosting a draft league draft, or just want a surprise squad for a random battle, this Pokémon team builder handles the ruleset — and the tactical analysis — so you can focus on playing. And whether you found it by searching for a pokemon random generator, a team randomizer, or a squad roller, it is all the same tool. Four things make it a real tool rather than a toy randomizer:

      Roll with real rules

      Every roll draws from all 1,025 species across Gen 1–9 with true random shuffling — no duplicates in a single team. Stack filters like a Nuzlocke ruleset: lock a generation, ban legendaries, limit evolution stages, or roll only shinies for a living-dex hunt.

      Put it to work for a Nuzlocke run in three clicks. Start a new challenge by locking the generation filter to the region you are playing — Gen 1 for Kanto, Gen 9 for Paldea — then hit Generate to roll your six starters. Strict Nuzlocke rulesets only allow one catch per route, so after a knockout, come back to the same filtered pool and roll a single replacement to keep the run legal. Set the evolution stage filter to Initial if you want unevolved encounters, or Final if you are building a post-game squad of fully evolved Pokémon.

      For casual random battles, skip the filters entirely: roll six Pokémon from the full 1,025-species pool and take whatever the random shuffle deals you. Or set a base stat total range — say 400 to 500 — to keep the fight balanced when you and a friend both roll squads and battle head-to-head. Want a faster, more dramatic pick? Try the Pokemon smash or pass game, where you judge random Pokémon one verdict at a time, or spin the Pokemon Wheel Spinner and let fate decide. Then test your memory with the Who's That Pokemon guessing game — a mystery Pokémon behind a silhouette, waiting to be named. When a catch deserves a proper identity, the Random Pokemon Name Generator turns any filtered pool into original nickname ideas. And if only a Mega Evolution will do, the Random Mega Pokemon Generator rolls nothing but megas — stat gains over each base form included. Hunting sparkles instead? The Random Shiny Pokemon Generator rolls only shiny-coloration Pokemon, with a regular-art compare switch on every card. Curious about the real hunt behind the sparkle? Pokemon Shiny Odds breaks down every rate from full-odds 1/8192 to the stacked 1/512, calculator included.

      Filter down to the exact pool

      Ten-plus filter dimensions combine freely: 18 types, 9 generations, base-stat-total range, Pokédex color, regional forms, rarity, and evolution stage. The pool counter updates live, so you always know exactly how many Pokémon your next roll can produce.

      Combine dimensions to find exactly the Pokémon you want. Chasing a water-electric favorite for a Kanto team? Toggle Gen 1 plus the Water type and the pool instantly narrows to candidates like Lapras and Gyarados. Running a draft league where legendaries are banned? Flip Legendary and Mythical to Exclude and the pool drops to legal picks only — the counter updates with every toggle, so you never guess how many candidates are left.

      Beyond competitive play, the filters power collection challenges too. Filter by Pokédex color to hunt a full shiny living dex color by color, switch the rarity dropdown to Only to summon mythical event Pokémon for a themed battle, or set Shiny mode to Random (8% odds per slot) for a shiny-hunt-style roll where every card has a chance to sparkle.

      Share the exact squad

      Every filter — and your rolled team — lives in the URL. Copy the config link from this random Pokémon generator and friends, draft-league rivals, or stream chat see your exact squad, shinies included, then roll their own from the same rules. Every roll is saved to your history with its own share link.

      This is what makes it a genuinely social team builder. Streamers and Discord communities use the share link constantly: roll a squad on stream, drop the link in chat, and every viewer opens the exact same six Pokémon with the same filters — shinies included. Friends can re-roll from your ruleset and compare team grades side by side. In Nuzlocke communities the link doubles as proof of a roll: share which Pokémon you caught and which filter pool you were playing under.

      Your Roll History (stored locally, up to 20 squads) keeps every team one click away. Hover any Pokémon in history to preview its stats, click a squad to reload it exactly as rolled, and use the per-squad Share button to re-post a team to your group at any time.

      Instant tactical report

      Every squad gets an instant tactical breakdown: an S–D team grade, an 18-type defensive matrix, STAB offensive coverage, average base stats, per-Pokémon role tags, and shared-weakness warnings with actionable tips. This random Pokémon generator doesn't just pick your team — it grades it.

      Read the report the way a competitive player does. The defensive matrix counts, for each of the 18 attacking types, how many of your members are weak to it — any row with 2 or more is a real hole, because a single opposing attacker can punch through it. The STAB coverage grid shows which defending types your squad hits super-effectively; grayed chips are blind spots worth patching with a coverage pick. The role tags (sweeper, wall, tank, speedster) make imbalance obvious at a glance: if every member is a sweeper and none is a wall, re-roll with a higher BST minimum or use the type filters to pull in a bulky defensive Pokémon.

      Run the roll-to-report loop a few times and the generator becomes a real team-building tool rather than a random button: each report tells you exactly what to change in your filters for the next roll — a fast Pokémon if your average Speed is under 60, a Ghost or Steel pick if your shared weaknesses are piling up.

      Every card also doubles as a pocket pokemon type coverage calculator: click any Pokémon and its detail card lists what it is weak to, what it resists, and what it is immune to, computed from the same 18-type chart. Whether you are checking a single pick for a gym or patching one hole in a six-Pokémon squad, the coverage row turns theory into a two-second read. When you want full manual control of a squad instead of a coin flip, the Pokemon Team Picker lets you hand-pick all six members and check the team's defensive matchups before you roll anything.

      FAQ

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Can I use it for Nuzlocke challenges?

      Absolutely. Filter by generation, type, evolution stage, or rarity to roll legal encounters, replacement Pokémon after a death, or a full randomized starter team.

      Which Pokémon are included?

      Every species from Generation 1 through Generation 9 — 1,025 Pokémon plus regional and special forms — with official artwork, base stats, abilities, and evolution data sourced from PokeAPI.

      Can I share the exact Pokémon squad I rolled?

      Yes. The Copy Config Link button encodes both your filters and your current squad — shiny variants included — into one URL. Anyone opening it sees the identical Pokémon, and every entry in your Roll History has its own Share button too.

      Does this random Pokémon generator save my rolls?

      Yes. Every squad you generate is stored locally in your browser's Roll History (up to 20 rolls) — hover any Pokémon to preview its details, click a roll to reload that exact team, or share it with a unique link. Nothing is uploaded or tracked.

      Does the generator analyze my team?

      Yes. Every roll produces a Team Tactical Report: an S–D grade, an 18-type defensive coverage matrix, STAB offensive coverage, average base stats, per-Pokémon role tags (sweeper, wall, tank…), and warnings about shared weaknesses with actionable tips.
