Pokemon stats and facts

Pikachu Pokemon: Stats, Weaknesses & Best Uses

What is Pikachu?

Pikachu is the Electric-type mouse Pokemon of Gen 1 and the face of the entire franchise. It was not designed to be the mascot; the anime picked a Pikachu as Ash's partner in 1997 and the popularity snowballed from there. Today it appears in every game, every crossover, and a surprising amount of merchandise that has nothing to do with battles.

In the games the public-facing Pikachu is deliberately approachable. It learns Thunderbolt early in most titles, its Speed is serviceable, and its evolution line is one of the first ones a new player will see. That accessibility is a design choice, not a limitation, so the competitive community has had to make it work against its own stat total for decades.

Pikachu stats, explained

320 base stat total with 90 Speed and 55 Attack. Special stats at 50 each are nothing special, but the Speed is genuinely useful: at level 50 a base-90 Speed stat beats most of the mid-tier attackers and most fully evolved Pokemon. The problem is everything else. Pikachu needs a Light Ball to reach real damage numbers; with one, its Attack and Sp. Atk double, and at that point Thunderbolt turns into a real threat.

Without the Light Ball, Pikachu is a status machine. Static gives a 30% chance to paralyze a contact attacker, and with a faster Thunder Wave or Nuzzle, Pikachu can cripple two threats before it goes down. That is the honest use case: not the damage, but the disruption.

Pikachu beyond the games

No Pokemon has lived a bigger double life. The anime's Pikachu is a battler with a personality, a friendship meter, and a refusal to evolve. The TCG's Pikachu gets a new base set card every single year. The games give it a Gigantamax form, an exclusive partner status in Let's Go, and a Light Ball item that doubles its Attack and Sp. Atk — proof that the developers want it playable in some games, even if the stat line says otherwise.

The world, meanwhile, made it a meme and a mascot in the same decade. It is the only Pokemon with a branded radio show, a guest appearance at the World Cup, and a Pokémon Center cafe in Tokyo. For a species whose national dex entry is a mouse that stores electricity in its cheeks, that is an extraordinary marketing run. In competitive play it is rarely seen, but the fans keep voting it back into the spotlight, and that is the real story of Pikachu.

If you want to use Pikachu seriously, the only realistic shell is doubles support or a Gen 1-style run where the manual dex is small and a 90-speed Thunderbolt beats most of the roster. Outside of that, treat it as the mascot it is: fun to roll, fun to look up, and a guaranteed smile in any Nuzlocke where the first encounter is a mouse with a lightning bolt.

And the site's Random Pokemon Picker rolls it more often than any other species, because a Pikachu draw always makes people laugh and check the page they just landed on.

Pikachu type matchups, read practically

One weakness, and it is the most common attack type in the games: Ground. Any Earthquake, any Dig, any sandstorm is a near-instant answer. The counterplay is simple: never lead with Pikachu against an Earthquake bot, and keep it out of the way until the Ground threats are gone.

The resistances do real work in casual play. Electric immunity means it can sit in front of a Jolteon, a Magnezone, or anything spamming Thunderbolt or Discharge, and the Flying resistance keeps Wing Attack users from making it sad. Steel resist covers the common Bullet Punch and Iron Head users. On a bench, Pikachu is a surprisingly good pivot against a quarter of the meta; it just should not be the one taking hits from the rest.

FAQ

Pikachu Pokemon FAQ

What type is Pikachu?

Pure Electric. It is weak only to Ground, resists Electric, Flying and Steel, and has no immunities. That makes Ground the single answer to Pikachu in almost every battle.

Is Pikachu good in battle?

It is middle-tier in casual play and rarely used at the top level. 90 Speed is the saving grace, but 320 base stat total and 55 Attack mean it wants a Light Ball more than a moveset. With a Light Ball it hits hard; without one, it mostly outruns things it cannot out-damage.

Does Pikachu evolve?

Yes. Pichu evolves into Pikachu when it levels up with high friendship, and Pikachu evolves into Raichu with a Thunder Stone. Raichu trades 20 Speed for 20 Sp. Atk and a much better stat distribution, but the games have made holding a Thunder Stone a choice, not an upgrade.

What is Pikachu's best ability?

Lightning Rod makes Electric moves deadly to Pikachu, raising its Sp. Atk as a bonus. Static is the ability you actually want on a casual playthrough: any contact move has a 30% chance to paralyze the attacker, which turns a Pikachu sitting on the bench into a poor-man's status spreader.

Why does the anime's Pikachu not evolve?

In the anime it is Ash's choice — the Pokemon repeatedly declines a Thunder Stone because it wants to stay Pikachu. The games preserve the choice too, since Raichu can never be renamed back to Pikachu once you evolve.
