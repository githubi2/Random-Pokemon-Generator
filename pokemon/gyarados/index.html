The Magikarp payoff

Gyarados Pokemon: Stats, Weaknesses and Battle Role

Meet Gyarados, the Atrocious Pokemon

Number 130 is a Water and Flying type from Generation 1, and it comes from a Magikarp at level 20. The abilities are Intimidate and Moxie, and the line finishes here with nothing evolving out of it.

Most players meet one as a story event rather than a wild encounter. The red Gyarados at the Lake of Rage is one of the most memorable scenes in the early games, and it is the first shiny most players ever see, whether they know what a shiny is at the time or not. That single encounter is why the recolour has a reputation well beyond its rarity.

Not a dragon, despite everything

The design reads as a dragon and the name suggests one, but the typing is Water and Flying. That matters in battle: it means Electric moves are a real problem and it means the Pokemon picks up the Ground immunity that the Flying half brings.

It is one of the more commonly mixed up species in the dex for exactly this reason. Anyone planning a matchup from memory rather than from the chart tends to guess Dragon and get the whole defensive sheet wrong.

Two weaknesses, five resistances

Electric and Rock are the types that do extra damage. Fire, Water, Fighting, Bug and Steel are all halved. Ground does nothing, thanks to the Flying typing, which gives it a free switch against Earthquake users.

Electric is the headline weakness, and on a physical attacker with 95 HP it is not something to shrug off. Rock is less common on strong attackers but shows up often enough on coverage moves to be worth tracking.

What the 540 total looks like

The Attack column carries everything: 125 is high enough that an unboosted hit hurts, and the rest of the spread supports it. 95 HP, 79 Defense and 100 Special Defense make it a physical attacker that can take a hit before it gives one back.

The two soft spots are 60 Special Attack and 81 Speed. Special sets are a waste of the stat line, and the speed means it will usually move second against anything fast, which is why it plays as a breaker rather than a sweeper. Everything it does well comes from the Attack column, so every set is built the same way at its core.

Intimidate and Moxie

Intimidate drops the opponent's Attack the moment Gyarados enters the field, which softens physical attackers before a single move is used. It is one of the best abilities in the game for a Pokemon that wants to trade hits, and it is on by default.

Moxie, the hidden ability, raises Attack every time the Pokemon knocks something out. Running it turns a strong attacker into a snowball, but it only pays off if the first hit lands the knockout, and the defensive benefit of Intimidate is gone.

Getting past the Magikarp stage

Magikarp is famously useless. It knows almost nothing worth using and its stat line is close to the worst in the dex, so the twenty levels before evolution are a genuine slog rather than a formality.

That is the trade this Pokemon offers: the worst possible start in exchange for a final form that arrives early, at level 20, and hits hard for the rest of the game. Few evolutions in the series have a wider gap between the two ends.

How it plays once it arrives

Gyarados is a physical breaker with a defensive cushion. Intimidate plus 100 Special Defense means it can come in more often than its typing suggests, and 125 Attack means the opponent has to respect every turn it stays on the field.

The answer to it is an Electric move, and every serious team carries one. Everything else has to work through five resistances and a Ground immunity to find a way in, which is why it has stayed relevant for so many generations.

The mega form, briefly

Gyarados has a mega evolution that changes the typing to Water and Dark. That swap removes the Electric weakness and trades in a new set of matchups, which makes the mega a different Pokemon to plan around rather than a straight upgrade.

It is worth knowing when comparing this page to older write-ups. Advice written for the mega form does not always apply to the standard one, and the two have very little overlap in how they should be played. Read the typing on whatever version you are actually using before planning a matchup around it.

FAQ

Gyarados Pokemon FAQ

What type is Gyarados?

Gyarados is a Water and Flying type, despite looking like a dragon. That combination gives it two weaknesses, five resistances and a complete immunity to Ground.

What is Gyarados weak to?

Electric and Rock are the two types that hit it for double damage. Fire, Water, Fighting, Bug and Steel all come back reduced, and Ground has no effect.

How does Gyarados evolve?

Magikarp becomes Gyarados at level 20. It is a plain level requirement with no item or trade involved, and nothing evolves out of Gyarados.

What abilities does Gyarados have?

Intimidate is the standard ability and Moxie is the hidden one. Intimidate lowers the opponent's Attack as it switches in, while Moxie raises its own Attack every time it knocks out an opponent.

What does the Gyarados evolution chart look like?

Two stages: Magikarp, then Gyarados at level 20. The chart ends there, so there is no third form to wait for.

What is Gyarados best at?

Physical damage with a defensive buffer. 125 Attack combined with 95 HP and Intimidate makes it a Pokemon that can come in, take a hit and hit back harder, though 81 Speed keeps it from being a sweeper.
