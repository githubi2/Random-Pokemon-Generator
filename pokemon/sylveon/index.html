Eevee's Fairy ending

Sylveon Pokemon: Stats, Weaknesses and Battle Notes

Meet Sylveon, the Intertwining Pokemon

Number 700 arrived in Generation 6 at the same time as the Fairy type itself, and it was the first new Eevee evolution in more than a decade. The typing is single Fairy, the abilities are Cute Charm and Pixilate, and the line ends here: it comes from an Eevee and evolves no further.

That timing matters for anyone reading older guides. Before Generation 6 there was no Fairy type, so no Sylveon, no Dragon immunity and no Steel weakness. Every matchup on this page is written against the modern chart, which is the one the games use now.

What the Fairy typing gives and takes

Two types hit it hard: Poison and Steel. Three come back reduced: Fighting, Bug and Dark. Dragon does absolutely nothing, which is the entry that made the typing famous when it was introduced as a check on the dragons that had been running the series.

Read as a defensive package, that is a good sheet with two clear holes. Poison and Steel are not the most common attacking types in the game, so Sylveon is rarely in real danger, and the Dragon immunity is worth more than its size suggests because it can switch into a Dragon move without taking anything at all.

Six columns, one clear shape

The spread leans special in both directions: 110 Special Attack and 130 Special Defense. Add 95 HP and you have a Pokemon built to take special hits and answer with its own, which is a role that fits neatly onto almost any team that needs a defensive pivot.

The physical side is thinner. 65 Defense and 65 Attack sit next to 60 Speed, so anything hitting it with a strong physical move is the real threat, and it will not be moving first against most of the dex. Sylveon does not cover every problem, it covers one problem extremely well.

Pixilate changes what its moves are

Pixilate, the hidden ability, turns every Normal-type move the Pokemon knows into a Fairy-type move and gives those moves a boost on top. Normal moves are common and cheap to learn, so the ability quietly widens its attacking options by rewriting them into its own type.

Cute Charm, the standard ability, has a chance to leave an attacker infatuated when it makes contact. It is unreliable by nature and does nothing against opponents that do not touch it, which is why the hidden option is the one most trainers chase.

Getting one out of an Eevee

The requirement has changed slightly between generations, but the shape of it has stayed the same: a strong bond with the trainer plus a Fairy-type move somewhere in the moveset. There is no stone and no level to hit, so this is a friendship evolution like Espeon and Umbreon before it.

It also makes Eevee the busiest family in the dex. Eight branches now run off one small brown Pokemon, split between stones, friendship, time of day, location and affection, and Sylveon is the one that needed a brand new type to exist.

Where it fits on a team

Sylveon is the special half of a defensive pair. Put it next to something that handles physical attackers and the two cover each other's bad side, which is the oldest team building trick in the game and still the one that works most reliably here.

It also gives a team an answer to Dragon-heavy opposition without spending a moveslot on it. Any Dragon-type attacker has to deal with a Pokemon that simply does not take damage from its best moves, and that alone can decide a matchup before a single turn is played.

What the numbers mean in a fight

Watch the physical column. A strong physical attacker that is not Fairy-weak will walk through the 65 Defense, so Sylveon wants to be brought in against special attackers and Ground, Water or Fighting types that cannot threaten it back.

Its own damage is steady rather than explosive. 110 Special Attack with a Fairy typing on top means it hits a lot of the dex for neutral or better, and with 130 Special Defense it will usually win any exchange that stays on the special side of the field. Just do not ask it to hold a line against a strong physical attacker, because 65 Defense will not manage that for long.

FAQ

Sylveon Pokemon FAQ

What type is Sylveon?

Sylveon is a pure Fairy type, which is the typing introduced in Generation 6. It brings two weaknesses, three resistances and a complete immunity to Dragon moves.

What is Sylveon weak to?

Poison and Steel both land for double damage. Fighting, Bug and Dark come back reduced, and Dragon attacks do nothing at all to it.

How does Sylveon evolve?

An Eevee becomes Sylveon once it has a strong bond with its trainer and knows a Fairy-type move. There is no stone and no level requirement, and nothing evolves out of Sylveon.

What abilities does Sylveon have?

Cute Charm is the standard ability and Pixilate is the hidden one. Pixilate turns Normal-type moves into Fairy-type moves and boosts them, while Cute Charm can infatuate an attacker that makes contact.

What does the Sylveon evolution chart look like?

One step: Eevee in, Sylveon out, by friendship with a Fairy move in the set. The chart stops there because Sylveon has no further evolution.

What is Sylveon best at?

Absorbing special attacks. 130 Special Defense and 95 HP make it a solid defensive pivot, and 110 Special Attack means it does not simply sit there while the opponent attacks.
