The headache Pokemon

Psyduck Pokemon: Stats, Weaknesses and Battle Notes

Meet Psyduck, the Duck Pokemon

Number 54 is a Water type with no second half, sitting at the start of a two-stage line that ends in Golduck at level 33. Damp and Cloud Nine are the standard abilities, and Swift Swim hides in the third slot.

The design is built on one joke that has lasted decades: a duck with a splitting headache, clutching its head, occasionally producing psychic effects it cannot control. The Pokedex entries have leaned into the headaches since the very first games, and the anime turned one of them into a long-running supporting character.

Three abilities, three different jobs

Damp prevents explosive moves from being used at all, which quietly shuts down a whole category of attacks. Cloud Nine suppresses every weather effect while it is on the field, the same way a flying legendary does, which is an unusually strong ability for a Pokemon at this level.

Swift Swim is the hidden option and doubles the Speed stat in rain. That is the one most trainers chase, since it turns a slow Pokemon into a fast one, but it needs weather support that the other two abilities do not. Pick it only when something else on the team is already bringing the rain.

What the 320 total looks like

65 Special Attack is the high mark, backed by 50 HP, 52 Attack, 48 Defense, 50 Special Defense and 55 Speed. The spread is flat and low, with no column that stands out enough to build around.

That flatness is the issue. There is no obvious role in the numbers, so the Pokemon's usefulness comes from its abilities and its typing rather than from anything in the stat line. It is a utility pick with a Water typing attached, and the sooner a trainer accepts that the easier the Pokemon is to use.

Weaknesses and resistances in full

Electricity and Grass are the two types that sting. Fire, Water, Ice and Steel all come back softer. Neither side of the chart carries an immunity, so there is no free switch in either direction.

Water is a solid defensive typing, and the four resistances here are all common attacking types, which is the reason a Pokemon with a 320 total can still be worth carrying. The two weaknesses are the standard pair for the type and nothing worse.

Growing into Golduck

Psyduck becomes Golduck at level 33, which is late for a first-stage Pokemon and means a long stretch with an unremarkable stat line. The final form keeps the Water typing and gains a much better spread to work with. Thirty-three levels is a real commitment for a Pokemon that spends most of them doing very little.

That gap is the trade. Psyduck is a Pokemon you carry for a while before it does anything, and the payoff is a solid Water type rather than a spectacular one. For a casual run that is usually enough.

Cloud Nine in practice

Suppressing the weather is the most interesting thing this Pokemon does. Against a rain team or a sandstorm setup, Cloud Nine turns off the entire strategy for as long as it stays on the field, which is the same effect a legendary ability has. Bringing it in at the right moment can end a fight that was already lost.

It does not make the Pokemon strong. It makes it inconvenient, and inconvenience is worth a slot more often than a mediocre stat line is.

Where the headaches come from

The lore is consistent across every generation: the headaches get worse over time, and when they peak the Pokemon produces psychic effects without meaning to. The design has never pretended to be serious, and the games have kept the gag alive for thirty years. It is a rare case of a joke that the mechanics do not contradict, which is probably why it has survived this long.

For a player, none of that changes the mechanics. It is a Water type with useful abilities and a low stat total, and it is best used for the two things it does well rather than for anything it does not. Slot it for the ability, keep it out of fights it cannot win, and it will earn its place on any team that needs either of those two effects. That is a narrow brief, and it is one the Pokemon fills better than most things at this total.

FAQ

Psyduck Pokemon FAQ

What type is Psyduck?

Psyduck carries the Water type and nothing else. Two weaknesses, four resistances, and no immunity on either side of the chart.

What is Psyduck weak to?

Electricity and Grass each do twice the damage. Fire, Water, Ice and Steel are halved instead, and no type is left immune either way.

How does Psyduck evolve?

Psyduck becomes Golduck at level 33. No item or trade is involved, and the final form keeps the same Water typing with a considerably better stat spread.

What abilities does Psyduck have?

Damp and Cloud Nine are the standard abilities, with Swift Swim as the hidden one. Damp blocks explosive moves, Cloud Nine suppresses all weather while it is on the field, and Swift Swim doubles its Speed in rain.

What does the Psyduck evolution chart look like?

Two stages: Psyduck, then Golduck at level 33. The line is a straight level-based evolution with nothing branching off it, and nothing evolves out of Golduck.

What is Psyduck best at?

Interfering with the opponent's plan. Cloud Nine cancels weather effects and Damp blocks explosive moves, so it is worth a team slot for the abilities rather than for its 320 base stat total.
