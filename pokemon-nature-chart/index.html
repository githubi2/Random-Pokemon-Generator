    How to choose

    Pokemon Nature Chart Guide: Picking the Right Nature

    Physical and special attackers

    The first question on any pokemon nature chart reading is which attack stat the species actually uses. A physical attacker with base 130 Attack and base 80 Sp. Atk should run Adamant (+Attack, −Sp. Atk) or Jolly (+Speed, −Sp. Atk), because the dropped stat is one it never touches — the 10% boost costs nothing worth having. Special attackers mirror the logic with Modest and Timid, which drop Attack instead. Dropping the offense a Pokemon never uses is the single most important habit in nature selection, and it is exactly what the finder above checks first.

    Speed or power: the real choice

    Adamant versus Jolly — and Modest versus Timid — is the debate every competitive player has. The Speed natures win when the boosted stat flips a specific matchup: a base 102 Speed Pokemon with Jolly outspeeds the entire unboosted base-100 tier, which often means striking first and never taking the hit at all. The power natures win when the team already controls speed through Tailwind, Sticky Web or priority moves, or when the extra damage turns a two-hit knockout into a one-hit knockout. If in doubt on a fast attacker, take the Speed nature — ties and near-ties are everywhere.

    Walls, tanks and Trick Room

    Defensive Pokemon boost whichever defense is stronger: Impish and Careful for walls that still use their Attack stat, Bold and Calm for supporters that can drop Attack entirely. Since no nature touches HP, boosting a defense is the only way natures add bulk. Trick Room flips the whole chart upside down — under Trick Room the slowest Pokemon moves first, so slow attackers actively want Brave or Quiet, which raise an attack stat while lowering Speed. A base-20-Speed Pokemon with a Brave nature and minimized Speed IVs is one of the scariest things in the format while the room is up.

    Mints, breeding and neutral natures

    Caught the perfect Pokemon with the wrong nature? Since Sword and Shield, mints rewrite the stat effect to match any nature you want — a Jolly Mint makes a Pokemon behave as Jolly without renaming its nature, and Scarlet and Violet sell them in regular shops. Breeders go further: a parent holding an Everstone passes its nature to every egg, so competitive players lock the nature in before EV training begins. The five neutral natures — Hardy, Docile, Serious, Bashful and Quirky — cancel themselves out and are fine for the story mode, but every serious team replaces them with a real boost.

    FAQ

    Pokemon Nature Chart FAQ

    What is a nature in Pokemon?

    A nature is a personality trait every Pokemon has, introduced in Generation 3 (Ruby and Sapphire). Most natures raise one stat by 10% and lower another by 10% — Adamant, for example, raises Attack and lowers Sp. Atk. Five natures (Hardy, Docile, Serious, Bashful and Quirky) are neutral and change nothing. There are 25 natures in total, and the chart above covers every one.

    How much does a nature change a Pokemon's stats?

    Exactly 10% in each direction: a boosted stat is multiplied by 1.1 and a lowered stat by 0.9, applied after the rest of the stat formula. On a fully trained level-50 attacker that works out to roughly 15 to 20 points in each direction — a 30-to-40-point gap between a boosting and a hindering nature. At competitive levels that gap decides who moves first and whether a hit knocks out.

    What are neutral natures?

    Hardy, Docile, Serious, Bashful and Quirky are the five neutral natures. Each one raises and lowers the same stat, so the effects cancel out and the Pokemon's stats stay exactly as the formula computes them. Neutral natures are perfectly fine for a casual playthrough, but competitive players treat them as a missed opportunity — a well-chosen +10% is pure upside when the dropped stat is one the Pokemon never uses.

    Do any natures raise or lower HP?

    No. Natures only touch five stats: Attack, Defense, Sp. Atk, Sp. Def and Speed. HP is never boosted or dropped by any nature. That is why defensive Pokemon invest in a Defense or Sp. Def nature such as Bold, Impish, Calm or Careful rather than looking for an HP boost that does not exist.

    How do I choose the best nature for my Pokemon?

    Start from the base stats. If Attack clearly beats Sp. Atk, run a physical nature that drops Sp. Atk — Adamant or Jolly. If Sp. Atk leads, drop Attack instead with Modest or Timid. Pick Jolly or Timid when the Pokemon is fast enough that the extra Speed flips important matchups, and Adamant or Modest when raw damage matters more. Walls boost their stronger defense, and slow Trick Room attackers actually want to lower Speed with Brave or Quiet. The finder above automates this for all 1,025 species.

    Can you change a Pokemon's nature?

    Not directly, but modern games have two workarounds. Mints, introduced in Sword and Shield, change the stat effect to match another nature without changing the nature name itself — a Pokemon fed a Jolly Mint behaves exactly as if it were Jolly. For breeding, a parent holding an Everstone passes its nature down to every egg, which is how competitive players lock in the nature they want before training.

    What nature is best for Garchomp?

    Jolly is the standard choice: base 102 Speed boosted by 10% lets Garchomp outspeed the crowded base-100 Speed tier, and the drop lands on Sp. Atk, which Garchomp never uses behind its base 130 Attack. Adamant is the common alternative on teams that already control speed — for example with Tailwind or Sticky Web — trading the Speed boost for noticeably harder hits.
