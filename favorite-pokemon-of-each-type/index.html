How it works

How to Pick Your Favorite Pokemon of Each Type

The rules are short: one favorite per type, eighteen slots, no wrong answers. Here is the whole walkthrough — picking, filling, downloading, and where a list like this travels once it leaves the browser.

One pick per type, eighteen in total

Start wherever the answer comes fastest. Click a slot — Water, Fire, whatever you are sure about — and the picker opens that type's full roster: every Pokemon in the dex that carries the type, from the famous names to the ones only you remember. Choose one and the slot locks in with its artwork, while the counter next to the buttons keeps score of what is left. The pools are honest to the type, too: Water is the deepest list at 192 entries, Ice the shortest at 69, and alternate forms like Megas and regional variants count where they belong.

Everything you click is saved in your browser immediately, so a refresh never costs you a decision. No login, no timer — the grid waits, and you can leave a hard type empty and come back to it tomorrow.

Why a grid beats a screenshot

The usual way to answer this question is to type eighteen names into a post and hope the formatting survives. Screenshots of a text list go blurry in a phone thread, retyping invites typos, and a typo in a favorites list is exactly how arguments start. This page hands you a finished image instead: a 1080 x 1080 PNG arranged in type order, with your trainer name across the top if you added one.

It is the same format people already paste into group chats and social feeds — except it starts clean, stays legible at any size, and never loses a slot to a line break. Download it once and it is yours to post, print, or keep as a bookmark of your taste.

How dual-type favorites work

Plenty of Pokemon hold two types at once, and the grid is built around that fact rather than against it. Every slot's list shows everything that carries the type, whether it is the Pokemon's first type or its second, so a Fire shortlist naturally includes Fire/Ground, Fire/Flying and everything in between. If the same Pokemon is genuinely your favorite in both of its types, let it take both slots — the chart still reads as one favorite per type, and a species is allowed to be the answer wherever it belongs.

That matches how the site's other tools treat types: the Random Pokemon Picker's "Type (any of)" filter reads a dual-type the same way, so the two pages never contradict each other.

Where your 18 favorites can go

A list like this is made to be shown. It works as a profile image, a pinned post, a greeting in a Discord server, or the opening move in a debate about which Water type actually deserves the slot. Add your trainer name before downloading and the grid carries your signature the way a card does.

And if someone asks how you made it, the share link rebuilds your exact eighteen picks on their screen — they can start from yours, argue with one slot, or clear it and build their own list from scratch.

FAQ

Favorite Pokemon of Each Type FAQ

What is a favorite Pokemon of each type list?

It is one pick for every type on the chart — the single Pokemon you would keep if each type could send only one. Eighteen answers work as a trainer signature: they say more about your taste than a top ten ever could.

How many Pokemon do I have to pick?

Eighteen, one per slot. A dual-type Pokemon can answer for both of its types if it is your top choice in each one, so a finished grid sometimes shows the same species twice — that is the rule, not a bug.

Do I have to fill all 18 types?

No. The grid saves with whatever you have filled, and empty slots render as dashed outlines with the type name, so a partial chart still looks deliberate. Stuck on a type you never use? The random fill completes the gaps from that type's own pool.

Can I change a pick after selecting it?

Any time. Open the slot again and choose differently — the old pick steps aside, the rest of the grid is untouched, and nothing is locked in until you download.

How do I download my favorite Pokemon of each type as an image?

Press Download PNG. The grid is drawn at 1080 x 1080 — square, so it posts to X, Instagram or a Discord thread without cropping — and the file is generated on your device. Nothing is uploaded, and no account is involved.

Can I share my 18 favorites with someone?

Press Copy My Favorites and you get a link that rebuilds your exact picks, trainer name included, on any device. Friends can open it to see your grid or clear it and start their own.

Is this tool free? Do I need an account?

Free, and no account. The grid, the PNG download and the share links all run in your browser, which is also why your picks stay on your device until you send the link or save the image.
