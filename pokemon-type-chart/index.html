How it works

How the Pokemon Type Chart Works

Read the matchup grid in one line

The Pokemon type chart is a 18-by-18 grid: attacking types run down the left, defending types across the top, and each cell holds the damage multiplier that results when the two meet. Values are always one of four: 2x (super effective), 1x (neutral), ½x (not very effective), or 0x (immune). Hover or tap any cell and the matchup is written out beneath the table, so you never have to decode the symbols.

Because the chart only stores the non-neutral matchups internally, the grid stays honest — every cell is computed from the same ruleset, which means the table can never drift from the calculator below it.

Find any type's weaknesses in seconds

Each of the 18 type cards under the grid lists three things: what the type is weak to, what it resists, and what it is immune to. Steel resists ten types and ignores Poison; Ground is immune to Electric; Ghost never touches Normal. That single card answers "what hits this type hard?" instantly — which is exactly what you want before a raid, a gym fight, or a competitive match.

Pair the type cards with the Pokemon Nature Chart and the Pokemon Shiny Odds pages to turn a good match-up into a well-built Pokemon: the nature chart picks the stat spread, the odds page plans the hunt, and this chart checks the damage math on every member of the squad.

Dual-type damage, computed exactly

Most Pokemon have two types, so the calculator is the part that actually matters. Pick an attacking type and one or two defending types — the result multiplies the individual matchups together. Fire vs Water/Steel is ½x × ½x = ¼x, Ice vs Dragon/Flying is 2x × 2x = 4x, and Electric vs Water/Ground is 0x: immunity beats every other rule. A 4x or ¼x result almost always decides a battle, so checking before you commit a move is habit-worthy.

Want to build a team that covers these holes? The Pokemon Team Picker maps your whole squad's defensive coverage, and the Random Pokemon Generator rolls one with a tactical report attached — grade, coverage matrix, and weakness warnings.

Learn the chart without memorizing it

Nobody memorizes 324 cells, and you do not have to. Three rules cover most of it. First, water beats fire, fire beats grass, grass beats water: the starter triangle is the skeleton of the whole chart. Second, three attacking types play offense against most of the dex: Ground, Fighting and Rock. Third, the weird ones are worth learning on purpose: Normal cannot touch Ghost, Electric cannot touch Ground, and Fairy swallows Dragon entirely.

When a matchup still surprises you, that is what the grid is for. Ten seconds of scrolling beats a minute of guessing, and after a few matches the patterns stick. Generations of players have played with partial knowledge; the ones who improve are the ones who check the chart after a surprising result instead of shrugging it off.

FAQ

Pokemon Type Chart FAQ

What is a Pokemon type chart?

A Pokemon type chart is the reference table that maps every attacking type against every defending type, showing the damage multiplier that results: 2x super-effective, 1x neutral, 0.5x not very effective, and 0x immune. It is the ruleset behind every battle in the games.

How many Pokemon types are there?

18 types: Normal, Fire, Water, Electric, Grass, Ice, Fighting, Poison, Ground, Flying, Psychic, Bug, Rock, Ghost, Dragon, Dark, Steel and Fairy. The Stellar Tera type added in Generation 9 is a Tera-crystal property, not a regular type on the chart.

How does dual-type damage work?

Multiply the two multipliers together. A Flying/Dragon target takes 4x from Ice (2x x 2x), a Water/Steel target takes 0.25x from Fire (0.5x x 0.5x), and any immunity zeroes the result — an Electric move on a Water/Ground Pokemon is 0x even though Water would be 2x.

What does STAB mean?

Same-Type Attack Bonus: a move matching the user's own type deals 1.5x damage on top of the type chart multiplier. A Water-type Pokemon using Surf deals 2x (chart) plus STAB, which is why same-type moves are usually the best coverage choice.

Has the type chart changed over the generations?

Yes. Generation 2 added Dark and Steel, with Ghost and Steel rebalanced; Generation 6 introduced Fairy and changed Ghost and Dark to neutral against Steel. Abilities like Levitate, Wonder Guard and Soundproof also override certain matchups.

How do I use this chart?

Hover or tap any cell in the grid to see the matchup written out, use the calculator to test any attacking type against one or two defending types, or open a type card below the grid to scan its weaknesses, resistances and immunities at a glance.

Which types are best defensively?

Steel is the standout: ten resistances plus a Poison immunity, weak only to Fire, Fighting and Ground. Electric and Water also carry several useful resistances. Offensively, Ground, Fighting and Rock hit the most types super effectively — which is why they are the staple coverage types on competitive sets.

Is the chart identical in every game?

Since Generation 6 the 18-type chart has stayed stable, so the same grid applies from X/Y through Scarlet and Violet. Game-specific quirks come from abilities and mechanics, not the chart: Levitate, Lightning Rod, Soundproof, Wonder Guard and Normalize all override matchups in specific situations.

What about the Tera type in Generation 9?

Tera types are a battle mechanic, not a permanent typing: a Pokemon Terastallizes once per battle and only one of its Tera types applies during that turn. The chart itself stays the same — Tera only changes which of the 18 types is in effect, so the grid still tells you the multiplier.
