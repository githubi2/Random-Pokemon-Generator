    How it works

    How to Play Who's That Pokemon

    The classic guess-the-pokemon game

    Who's That Pokemon is the guessing game every Pokemon fan grew up with: a mystery Pokemon appears, hidden behind a visual clue, and you try to name it. This version keeps the pure loop — one Pokemon, three tries, hints when you miss — and turns it into a fast little quiz you can play between rounds of the random Pokemon generator, at a party, or as a quick knowledge check with friends.

    Play it in three steps

    Step one: the game deals a random Pokemon and hides it — as full artwork on Easy, a dark silhouette on Normal, a blocky pixel sprite on Hard, or nothing but a cry on Elite. Step two: type your guess into the box, or pick a suggestion if the autocomplete catches it. Step three: hit Guess, or tap I don't know to reveal the answer right away. You have 3 attempts per round, and the next Pokemon appears the moment you're done.

    Four difficulty modes, one mystery

    Easy shows the Pokemon's full official artwork — a warm-up mode for kids and casual fans. Normal hides the Pokemon behind a black silhouette; the shape alone has to spark your memory. Hard uses a blocky pixel silhouette that strips away the outline detail, so you have to read the pose and proportions. Elite shows no image at all — only the Pokemon's cry plays, and your ears do the work. Switch difficulty any time in the Settings tab; the next round uses the new mode.

    Hints: learn to read the clues

    Every wrong guess unlocks a progressive hint. On Easy and Normal the first miss reveals the Pokemon's type badges; miss again and you get the generation and the first letter of the name. Hard mode holds the generation hint back longer, and Elite is stricter still — the type appears, but the first letter only shows up on your second miss. Use the hints the way speedrunners do: identify the types first, narrow the generation, then let the first letter seal it. The same reading skills make you faster at the Pokemon smash or pass verdicts.

    Play with the whole roster

    By default the quiz draws from the full Gen 1-9 roster — every region, every generation, regional forms included. Prefer a focused run? Open Settings and set the generation to Gen 1 for a Kanto-only round, or Gen 4 if you grew up with Sinnoh. The pool regenerates with each round, and the same Pokemon can appear twice in a session, so nothing runs out.

    Listen for the cry

    Every round has a Play Cry button, and it is more than a hint: Pokemon cries are distinctive, and an Elite run is a genuine listening challenge. Correct and incorrect guesses play a short sound effect too — toggle both in Settings if you are playing quietly. Whether you enjoy the Pokemon Wheel Spinner or a heavy Nuzlocke challenge with the Nuzlocke Team Builder, the cry is one of the quickest ways to recognize a Pokemon.

    Track your streak

    The Stats tab keeps a running record: rounds played, correct-guess percentage, average and fastest time per round, plus your current streak and best streak. Streaks reset on a wrong guess or a give-up, so a clean run is something to brag about. Everything saves automatically in your browser — close the tab, come back tomorrow, and your best streak is still there. Reset Stats wipes the record whenever you want a fresh start.

    Who's That Pokemon for streams and classrooms

    This game is built for quick rounds: three tries, snappy hints, no setup, runs in any browser. Streamers use it as a filler between matches — chat shouts guesses and the first correct call wins. Teachers use the silhouette and pixel modes as a warm-up that gets kids reading visual details. And because it needs no account and no install, a whole class can take turns on a single screen. Keep the same playful energy going with the smash or pass verdicts — the verdict buttons make it a fast party game too.

    Play it with friends: the race variant

    The quiz becomes a party game the moment two people share a screen. Run it as a race: deal a round, shout your guess, first correct call wins the point — the loser takes the next round as the host. Streamers turn it into a chat vote: viewers type their guess, and the correct viewer names get a shout-out. Classrooms use the same loop for a quick warm-up, letting one student at a time take the seat while everyone else whispers hints. Play a five-round set on Normal to warm up, then switch to Hard and let the pixels do the damage; the round timer turns every guess into a stake, since a slow answer is a wrong answer once the next player is waiting.

    What your score means

    Half the fun is comparing notes. A correct rate above 70% with an average time under 10 seconds usually means you are reading silhouettes by shape, not guessing. A slow and steady player often beats a fast one on Hard mode, where detail is stripped away. And if your best streak is double digits on Elite, you have officially memorized the cry library.     FAQ

    Who's That Pokemon FAQ

    What is Who's That Pokemon?

    Who's That Pokemon is a guessing game: a mystery Pokemon is hidden behind a silhouette, pixel art, or just its cry, and you type your guess. You have three attempts per round, with progressive hints after each wrong guess.

    How do I play the Who's That Pokemon game?

    A Pokemon appears hidden behind a visual clue. Type your guess into the search box or pick from the suggestions, then hit Guess. You have 3 attempts to name it right; wrong guesses unlock hints like the type, generation, and first letter.

    What are the difficulty modes?

    Easy shows the full artwork, Normal hides the Pokemon behind a dark silhouette, Hard uses a blocky pixel silhouette with less detail, and Elite shows no image at all — only the Pokemon's cry plays.

    Can I filter the puzzle by generation?

    Yes. Open the Settings tab and pick any generation from Gen 1 to Gen 9, or keep All Gens to draw from the complete 1,025-species roster plus regional forms.

    Does the game include Pokemon cries?

    Yes. Every round has a Play Cry button, and in Elite mode the cry is your only clue. Sound effects for correct and incorrect guesses can be toggled in Settings.

    Are my stats saved?

    Yes — your stats (correct guesses, accuracy, average and fastest time, streak and best streak) and settings are saved automatically in your browser. Use Reset Stats to start over.
