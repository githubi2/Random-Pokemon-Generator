    How the coverage check works

    Every attack type in Pokemon does one of three things to a defending type: double damage, half damage, or nothing at all. Coverage is the count of defending types your team can hit for double. A type is either covered or it is not, and the number of Pokemon that can cover it is what tells you how fragile that cover is.

    This calculator uses each Pokemon's own typing, which is the same-type attack bonus case, the strongest attack it can fire without spending a slot on a coverage move. A Charizard contributes Fire and Flying. Those two types together hit 5 of the eighteen defending types for double damage (Grass, Ice, Fighting, Bug, Steel), and nothing else.

    Dual-type defenders multiply. A move that is 2x against one half and 0.5x against the other lands at 1x, and 2x against both halves gives 4x. That is why the grid is about attacking types and the defensive panel is about members: the two questions are answered by different maths.

    What a coverage gap actually costs

    A gap is a defending type nothing on your team hits for double damage. It does not mean those matchups are lost, it means every turn against them is a normal-damage turn. A bulky Normal-type wall takes twice as many hits as a Pokemon that is weak to something on your team, which is the difference between a two-turn knock-out and a five-turn one.

    Not every type is equally easy to break. Normal can only be hit for double by 73 of the 1,025 species, Electric by 75. Both lists are short because the chart is not the problem, the typing pool is: very few species carry a Normal or Electric attack as part of their own type.

    Rock is the opposite: 463 species hit it for double damage, so almost any team covers it by accident. When you are choosing between two members for the last slot, cover a hard type with the pick rather than doubling up on an easy one.

    Four Pokemon can hit all 18 types

    Run a greedy search over the dex and it takes 4 Pokemon to cover every type with same-type attacks: Swinub, Scraggy, Zubat, Bulbasaur. That is the floor. It is also a warning, because those four are chosen purely for offence and the defensive side of that team is a disaster.

    The defensive panel in the calculator is there to catch exactly this. A team that covers all 18 types usually stacks shared weaknesses, and once two or three members share one, a single opponent of that type threatens most of the squad. In practice most good teams leave two or three types uncovered and spend the slots on resisting what the metagame throws.

    The honest way to use this tool: add your six, look at the gaps, and decide whether each gap is one you can patch with a single coverage move. If it is, the gap is cheap. If it is a type nothing on the team learns, it is a real hole.

    Pokemon Type Coverage Calculator FAQ

    What does type coverage mean in Pokemon?

    Type coverage is the list of defending types your team can hit for double damage. A move deals 2x against a type it beats, 0.5x against types that resist it and 0 against types immune to it, so a team is covered on a type when at least one member can hit it for 2x.

    How many Pokemon do you need to cover all 18 types?

    With same-type attacks only, 4 is enough: Swinub, Scraggy, Zubat, Bulbasaur. That is a floor, not a good team, because those picks leave huge defensive holes, but it shows how little it takes to hit every type for double damage.

    Which type is hardest to hit super effectively?

    Normal tops the list: only 73 of the 1,025 species can hit it for 2x with their own typing, against 463 that can hit rock. The chart is not unfair, there are simply very few normal attackers in the dex.

    Does this calculator use moves or species typing?

    Species typing: each Pokemon contributes its own one or two types, which is what a same-type attack bonus move looks like in practice. If you run a coverage move of a different type, note it separately, because that is the usual way teams patch a gap.

    What is a coverage gap and why does it matter?

    A gap is a defending type nothing on your team hits for 2x. Against that type every attack is neutral at best or resisted at worst, so a bulky opponent of that type takes twice as many turns to break down. Gaps cost turns, not games, but turns are what stall teams run on.

    How do dual-type Pokemon work in the chart?

    Multipliers stack. A move that is 2x against one of the defender's types and 0.5x against the other lands at 1x, and 2x against both gives 4x. That is why a single 4x weakness on a dual type is worth as much as two separate 2x weaknesses.

    Should I pick coverage or defence?

    Both, and they pull in opposite directions. The greedy four-Pokemon cover above hits all 18 types but stacks Fire (2), Ice (2), Fighting (2) weaknesses across the team. Real teams usually accept two or three gaps to keep the defensive side sane.

    Do abilities and items change coverage?

    They can. Levitate removes Ground damage, Flash Fire absorbs Fire, and abilities like Pixilate turn earlier-generation moves into Fairy coverage. This calculator reads typing only, so treat it as the baseline and adjust for the ability your Pokemon actually runs.

    Is coverage different in Pokemon GO?

    Yes, in one big way: GO teams are three Pokemon, not six, and shields change when a 2x hit matters. The typing maths is identical, so a gap here is still a gap there, but three members cover far less ground.

    Can I save or share a team?

    The team in the address bar is the share link: the Copy result link button puts it on your clipboard, and opening that URL rebuilds the same six Pokemon.
