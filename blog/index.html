Editorial scope

What This Blog Covers

This is the written companion to the tool suite. The tools answer questions instantly — roll a team, compute a rate, check a nature — and the blog explains the reasoning underneath, so you can sanity-check any tool against first principles instead of trusting a black box.

Odds and probability

Shiny rates, catch math, roll distributions and the statistics of long hunts. We show the formulas, not just the answers: where 1/8192 comes from, why independent rolls mean nothing is ever "due," and how to read cumulative probability when you plan a 1,000-encounter session. Our first guide, Pokemon Shiny Odds Explained, covers the full ladder from full odds to the Masuda-plus-Charm ceiling, and it pairs directly with the interactive Pokemon Shiny Odds calculator.

Team-building strategy

Natures, roles, speed tiers and defensive synergy — the thinking that turns six strong Pokemon into one strong team. Expect deep dives on the nature system (the Pokemon Nature Chart page is the interactive companion), type-coverage planning with the Pokemon Team Picker, and squad construction theory you can apply whether you build by hand or start from a random roll.

Challenge runs and formats

Nuzlocke rulesets, mono-type runs, randomizer-style drafts and wheel-picked squads. We write about formats you can actually run with the tools on this site: roll encounters with the Nuzlocke Team Builder, draft from the Pokemon Wheel Spinner, or let the Random Pokemon Generator deal your fate, then read the strategy guides that make those runs winnable.

Recently Added to the Site

The blog launches alongside two major additions to the tool suite. Both were built from search data — real questions players ask — and both pair naturally with the guides we publish here. If you have not tried them yet, they are the fastest way to put this blog's advice into practice.

Pokemon Nature Chart

Our newest reference tool answers the question every competitive player asks after a catch: which nature should this Pokemon run? The Pokemon Nature Chart lists all 25 natures with their stat boosts and drops in a filterable, sortable table, and its finder reads any species' base stats to recommend a nature — physical, special, wall or Trick Room — with the real level-50 numbers attached. A full strategy guide on natures and speed tiers is on the editorial calendar, and it will draw directly from this tool.

Pokemon Shiny Odds

The Pokemon Shiny Odds page is half reference, half calculator: every base rate from Gen 2 to Gen 9, every boost method from the Shiny Charm to Legends: Arceus outbreaks, and an interactive panel that converts your exact hunt conditions into a fraction, a percentage and an expected hunt length. This week's guide, Pokemon Shiny Odds Explained, is the narrative companion — read the guide for the why, then open the calculator for your own numbers.

Our standard

How These Guides Are Written

Numbers before narrative

Every rate, base stat and mechanic we cite is verified against the game's actual systems — the same data that powers the tools on this site. When the community measures something empirically rather than reading it from the code, like Sword and Shield's battle-count bonus, we say so and label the confidence level. You will never find a recycled forum myth presented as fact here.

Every guide pairs with a tool

Reading about odds is useful; computing your own is better. Each article links the exact tool that puts its lesson into practice, whether that is the shiny calculator, the nature finder, or the squad builders. If you want the reverse path — tools first, theory later — start from the Random Pokemon Generator homepage and come back when a number surprises you.
