Pokemon Smash or Pass Tier List

I have judged hundreds of these picks, argued about them in group chats, and changed my mind on at least three Pokemon mid-sentence. This is the tier list that came out the other side: who gets an instant smash, who gets a reluctant pass, and the five decisions that nobody can make quickly.

Here is the thing about Pokemon Smash or Pass: everyone thinks their taste is obvious until the dex actually puts them on the spot. Sure, you'd smash Charizard. But then the game serves you a Probopass and you realize you have no philosophy, only vibes. This tier list is my attempt to build the philosophy — a working standard for the whole 1,025-species dex, from the ones you smash before the sprite even finishes loading to the ones that make you stare at the ceiling and question who you are.

Quick disclaimer before the comments section forms an angry mob: this is about design, charm, and the joy a Pokemon brings when it shows up on screen — nothing weird. Now let's get into it.

How I Judged These

Three questions, answered fast, no overthinking allowed:

1. Does the design commit? Great Pokemon have one strong idea executed with confidence. Gengar is a grinning shadow. That's it, that's the pitch, and it's perfect. Bad Pokemon are committee meetings — too many details fighting each other, or one joke stretched past breaking point.

2. Does it have presence? Some Pokemon walk into a route and own it. Arcanine has presence. Bunnelby does not. Presence is partly stats and lore, but mostly it's silhouette. If I can recognize the thing from its shadow — and yes, Who's That Pokemon is a legitimate research method for this — it has presence.

3. Would I smile if it appeared right now? This is the tiebreaker and honestly the most important one. Nostalgia counts. A Pokemon that made your childhood save file better gets credit for it, forever. I won't apologize for that.

S Tier: Instant Smash

Lucario. The aura dog that became a Smash Bros. mainstay for a reason. Clean lines, cool typing, and it somehow looks both ancient and futuristic. Easiest smash in the dex.

Mimikyu. A lonely ghost wearing a handmade Pikachu costume because it just wants friends. That's not a Pokemon, that's a Pixar short. The broken-neck disguise detail is the single best visual gag Game Freak ever committed to a sprite.

Gengar. Gen 1's final boss of cool. Twenty-nine years of new designs and nobody has topped the purple menace. The Mega and Gigantamax forms are great, but base Gengar grinning at you from a wall shadow is untouchable.

Arcanine. It's a legendary fire dog that was almost literally classified as a Legendary. Every Pokedex entry reads like mythology. If Arcanine appeared in front of you in real life, you would cry a little. Smash.

A Tier: Strong Yes

Dragonite. The original pseudo-legendary is a big friendly dragon mailman, and the contrast between its goofy face and its ability to fly around the planet in 16 hours is exactly the kind of commitment I asked for. Docked half a tier because Dragonair is prettier and everyone knows it.

Sylveon. The best Eeveelution, and I'll defend the ribbon-flesh debate: they're feelers, they're cute, move on. Greninja. A frog ninja with a tongue scarf should be stupid. Instead it's the coolest starter final form ever designed, and the entire Kalos anime was basically its highlight reel. Tyranitar. A kaiju. They just made a kaiju. Unambiguous.

B Tier: Reliable, Not Exciting

Welcome to the tier that starts arguments. Charizard lands here, and before you close the tab: Charizard is a very good orange dragon. It's also the most overexposed Pokemon in history, with two Megas, a Gigantamax, and a permanent seat in every TCG set at prices that suggest it pays Game Freak's rent. The design is a B. The brand is an S. Average it out.

Pikachu gets the same treatment — iconic beyond measurement, but as a design it's a round yellow mouse. Snorlax is beloved, blocked my path in 1998, and has been coasting on that ever since. Gardevoir is elegant and genuinely interesting as a concept, and loses points only because the internet has made it impossible to discuss normally. You know what you did.

C and D Tier: Where Friendships End

Zubat is a C only because Crobat redeems the family; the base form is a cave tax you pay in encounters. Trubbish is a garbage bag, and I respect the honesty but I don't have to smash it. Stunfisk — look. The derp face is funny exactly once. It has been eleven generations. Bunnelby exists to be the first thing you catch and the first thing you box.

D tier is harsher. Luvdisc is a pink heart whose only real function was holding Heart Scales, an item about move relearning — a Pokemon whose design brief was "inventory." Unown is alphabet soup with a creepy eye, twenty-eight forms of the same disappointment. Delibird is a Santa penguin whose signature move can heal the opponent, which is the funniest sentence in this article and the only good thing about it. Watchog looks like it's about to ask for the manager of Route 1.

The 5 Hardest Pokemon Smash or Pass Decisions

Every player eventually hits these. They're the picks where your gut and your brain file separate reports:

1. Pikachu. Smashing the mascot feels like saluting a flag. Passing it feels illegal. There is no clean answer, only the knowledge that millions of people have faced this exact paralysis in the game before you.

2. Eevee. Adorable, yes, but smashing Eevee feels like choosing one Eeveelution and betraying eight others. You're not judging a Pokemon, you're judging potential.

3. Gardevoir. A graceful, loyal, genuinely well-designed Pokemon carrying three metric tons of internet baggage. Judge the design or judge the discourse?

4. Ditto. It can be any Pokemon. Is smashing Ditto smashing everything, or smashing a pink blob with a face drawn on? Philosophers are silent.

5. Magikarp. Useless flopping fish, or the prologue to Gyarados? You're really voting on whether you believe in comebacks.

Now Make Your Own

That's my list — wrong in at least four places, depending on who you ask. The whole point of Pokemon Smash or Pass is that the dex doesn't care about my criteria: it deals you whatever comes next, one species at a time, and you decide on instinct. Go run a session, keep your own tally, and see where your tier list diverges from mine. If you put Stunfisk in S tier I want to hear the reasoning. Actually, no I don't. But I respect the confidence.
