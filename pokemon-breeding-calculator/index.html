      How the Pokemon Breeding Calculator decides

      The whole check comes down to three questions, and every one of them is answered by data the games themselves use: the egg group of each parent, the gender each parent can be, and whether the species is allowed to produce eggs at all. Nothing here is a guess or a house rule.

      Egg groups are the family tree the breeding system actually runs on, and each species carries one or two of them. A shared group is the whole requirement on that front, so a Bulbasaur (Monster and Grass) happily pairs with a Charmander (Monster and Dragon) even though the two look nothing alike. If the two lists do not overlap, the pair produces nothing no matter how compatible they look on screen.

      Gender is the second gate. Each species has a possible gender set rather than a fixed one: some are always male, some always female, most can be either, and a handful are genderless. The calculator compares what each parent can be, which is why a pair still passes when you only own a male Eevee today. Genderless Pokemon fail the check outright and have to breed through Ditto instead.

      The third gate is the one people trip over most: the Undiscovered group. Legendaries, mythicals, baby Pokemon and most fossils sit there, and no pairing produces an egg. The result card names this explicitly instead of just saying no, so you are not left guessing whether you picked the wrong partner.

      Field is the group that matters most in practice: 278 species, a quarter of the dex, which is why so many breeding plans start with an Eevee or a Pikachu. Undiscovered is the second largest at 151, and it is entirely a list of Pokemon you cannot breed.

      Hatch steps: what the number actually means

      Every species carries a hatch counter, and the egg needs that many cycles of 255 steps. Bulbasaur sits at 20, so its egg takes about 5,100 steps; Dratini sits at 40, so its egg takes about 10,200. That is the only number the game uses, and it does not change with the parents, the daycare, or how many eggs you have already hatched.

      The one lever you have is an ability: Flame Body, Magma Armor and Steam Engine all halve the step count while that Pokemon is in the party. A Talonflame or Fletchinder is the usual pick because it also flies. The halving does not stack, so two Flame Body Pokemon are no faster than one.

      The calculator prints the step count for both parents so you know what you are signing up for before you commit to a breeding run. If you are hatching for a specific nature or a hidden ability, that number multiplied by the eggs you expect to go through is the real time cost of the project.

      What actually passes down from the parents

      Compatibility only decides whether an egg appears. What comes out of it is a separate set of rules, and they are worth knowing before you start because a few of them are the difference between ten eggs and a hundred.

      Nature passes down if either parent holds an Everstone, so a single Everstone locks the nature you want. Five of the six IVs pass if one parent holds a Destiny Knot, drawn at random from the twelve the pair carries between them, which is why breeders stack high IVs on both sides instead of one. Egg moves pass from the father in the older games and from either parent from Sword and Shield onward, and the mother's Pok Ball is what the egg inherits.

      None of this changes the compatibility answer, but all of it is why the pair you pick matters beyond the egg group check. If you want the odds side of hatching, the Pokemon Shiny Odds page covers the Masuda method and how the Shiny Charm stacks on top of it, and the Pokemon IV Calculator covers what the five inherited IVs are actually worth.

      When you have your pair and want a team around it, the Pokemon Team Picker and the Random Pokemon Picker both draw from the same 1,025 species this tool uses, and the Pokemon Pokedex has a page per species if you need the full stat line.

      Pokemon Breeding Calculator FAQ

      Can any two Pokemon breed?

      No. Both parents need at least one shared egg group, they need to be able to be opposite genders, and neither can sit in the Undiscovered group. Ditto is the one exception: it pairs with anything that is not Undiscovered.

      How do I know if two Pokemon share an egg group?

      Run them through the calculator above and read the two egg group lines. Bulbasaur is Monster and Grass, so it pairs with anything in Monster or Grass, from Charmander to Oddish. One shared group is enough.

      Why does the calculator say a legendary cannot breed?

      Legendaries and mythicals sit in the Undiscovered egg group, and so do baby Pokemon and most fossil Pokemon. Undiscovered means no egg is ever produced, whatever you pair it with.

      Can two Ditto breed?

      No, two Ditto produce nothing. Ditto exists to breed with other Pokemon: put it in the daycare with a compatible partner and the egg that appears is that partner's species.

      Can a male and a male breed?

      No. Breeding needs one Pokemon that can be male and one that can be female, which is why a genderless Pokemon such as Magnemite only breeds through Ditto. Pokemon like Eevee can be either, so the calculator reads the possible genders, not the one you happen to own.

      What does the step number mean?

      Every species has a hatch counter. Multiply it by 255 and you get the number of steps the egg needs. A 20-cycle egg such as Bulbasaur takes about 5,100 steps; a 40-cycle egg such as Dratini takes about 10,200.

      Does a Pokemon with Flame Body hatch eggs faster?

      Yes. Walking with Flame Body, Magma Armor or Steam Engine in the party halves the step count, which is why breeders keep a Talonflame or Fletchinder around. The halving applies once, not twice.

      Do the two parents have to be the same species?

      Only if you want the egg to hatch that same species. Cross-species pairs still produce eggs, and the egg always hatches the female parent's species, or the non-Ditto parent when one parent is Ditto.

      What carries over from the parents?

      Nature passes with an Everstone, five IVs pass with a Destiny Knot, and egg moves pass from the father in older games or from either parent from Sword and Shield onward. The mother's Pok Ball passes down as well.

      Is this the same as breeding in Scarlet and Violet?

      The egg group and gender rules have not changed since Gen 2. What changes between games is where you leave the parents, whether you need a Picnic, and how egg power works. The compatibility answer this tool gives holds in every game.
